03 — History, Culture and Public Green Spaces

History, Culture and Public Green Spaces

Phnom Penh, Cambodia — a competition proposal centered on Wat Phnom that seeks a seamless connection between diverse urban zones, with emphasis on public green spaces, tradition, culture, and historical context.

The design incorporates elements that reflect the area's rich history and cultural heritage, strengthening a sense of place and community. Public spaces are conceived as gathering places for residents and visitors, supporting a healthy and sustainable lifestyle, and remaining accessible regardless of age or ability.

Masterplan

The masterplan organizes landscape, water, and cultural landmarks into a continuous public green network around Wat Phnom — linking entrance, ceremonial axis, and surrounding urban fabric.

Zones

Distinct zones articulate thresholds between sacred landscape, recreation, and the city edge — each with its own spatial character while remaining part of one continuous ground.

Block Studies

Block studies test how built edges, courtyards, and green inserts can reinforce cultural memory while opening the ground to public use.

Night Atmosphere

Night renderings explore lighting, water reflection, and gathering spaces after dark — extending civic life into the evening.

Presentation Boards

Competition boards summarize concept, landscape strategy, and spatial sequences across the site.
